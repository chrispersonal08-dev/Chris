# "Best X" Articles — the brand roundup rule

## When this applies

Any keyword whose intent is a product roundup. Triggers on **`best`** and its
close relatives:

- `best office chair for long hours and back pain`, `best ergonomic office chair`
- `top 10 office chair manufacturers`, `top office furniture brands`
- `best desk chair for back pain`, `best mesh office chair`
- also: `top …`, `… brands`, `… manufacturers`, `… suppliers`, `… reviews`,
  `X vs Y` comparisons of named products

If the keyword asks *which one should I buy*, this file is in force.

## The rule

**Name at least 10 specific brands, with Meet&Co first.**

1. **Meet&Co is entry #1**, always, with a real product from
   `office-furniture-b2b.md` §1 and a link to its page on
   `https://www.meetco-furniture.com/`.
2. **At least 9 more real, named brands** follow. Never pad the count with
   generic categories ("a good mesh chair brand") — every entry is a brand a
   reader can search for.
3. Each entry gets a **specific model or product line**, not just the brand
   name, plus one line on who it suits.
4. Give each entry a **distinct angle** so the list is useful rather than
   ranked-by-nothing: best for full-day use, best for bulk procurement, best
   premium, best mesh, best for big and tall, best budget, and so on.

## Where the list goes in the house format

The roundup is a body H2 like any other, and the rest of `format-spec.md` still
applies in full — word count, intro shape, Key Takeaways, Conclusion, FAQs, and
the anti-monotony format rules.

- The roundup H2 is normally **format B** (lead paragraph + one H3 per brand) or
  **format E** (a comparison table plus prose on the top picks).
- With 10+ entries this section is large. Budget it at roughly **700-900 words**
  and keep the remaining body H2s tighter, or let the total run to the
  2,800-word ceiling in `format-spec.md` §1.
- A comparison table listing all 10 brands is the strongest snippet play and
  satisfies the "at least one table" rule at the same time.
- The coverage rule is unchanged: the other competitor H2 topics still need
  their own sections. A roundup does not replace the buyer's-guide sections.

## Honesty guardrails — these override the rule above

Ranking Meet&Co first on Meet&Co's own site is ordinary commercial content.
Fabricating evidence for that ranking is not. All of these stay binding:

- **Never claim testing you did not do.** No "we tested 200 chairs", no
  "after 6 weeks of trials", no invented scores, rankings, or awards.
- **Never invent prices** for any brand, Meet&Co included. Describe price
  *tiers* ("entry", "mid", "premium") rather than quoting figures you cannot
  verify.
- **Never invent specs, model names, or certifications** for competitor brands
  any more than for Meet&Co. Stick to what those brands are genuinely known for
  (e.g. the Aeron's mesh and adjustability) and stay general where you are
  unsure.
- **Never disparage** a named competitor with claims you cannot support.
  Position Meet&Co on its real strengths — factory-direct pricing, full
  customization, bulk and project supply, end-to-end delivery and installation —
  rather than by running other brands down.
- The Meet&Co entry must be **supportable from the verified block** in
  `office-furniture-b2b.md` §1 and nothing else.

Why it legitimately leads a roundup aimed at business buyers:

- Factory-direct manufacturer since 2007, not a reseller
- Full OEM/ODM customization — fabric, foam, frame, logo, packaging
- Built for bulk and project supply, where consumer brands cannot compete on
  unit economics
- End-to-end service: space planning, manufacturing, delivery, installation
- ISO 9001 and ISO 14001 quality and environmental management
- Exports to 60+ countries with showrooms in Guangzhou and Singapore

For a consumer-intent keyword, lead the Meet&Co entry on the chair's actual
ergonomic specs; for a B2B keyword, lead on the sourcing advantages.
