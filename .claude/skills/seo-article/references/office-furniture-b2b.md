## 2. Who is reading

| Persona | What they search | What they need in the article |
|---|---|---|
| **Importer / wholesaler** | supplier, wholesale, bulk, factory direct | MOQ, unit economics, container math, payment terms |
| **Distributor / dealer** | brands, price list, dealer, catalog | Margin structure, product range, warranty, spare parts |
| **Project / contract buyer** | office fit-out, bulk order, commercial | Lead time vs project schedule, batch consistency, on-site delivery |
| **Interior contractor / designer** | dimensions, layout, materials, finishes | Specs, finish options, space planning, custom sizing |
| **Corporate procurement** | ergonomic standards, compliance, best | Certification, ergonomics evidence, TCO, warranty terms |

Match the persona to the keyword's intent. "Office chair supplier" is an
importer query; "best ergonomic office chair for back pain" is an end-user
query even on a B2B site — write it for the end user and place the trade angle
in the closing sections only.

## 3. Trade angles that differentiate

These are the H2/H3 topics generic competitors omit. Use the ones that fit;
never bolt all of them onto every article.

- **MOQ** — per model, per color, per container; how MOQ moves with
  customization; mixed-model container loading
- **OEM / ODM** — logo placement, custom fabric and foam density, private label
  packaging, tooling cost and amortization, exclusive design terms
- **Lead time** — sampling (7-15 days), mass production, peak season
  (pre-Canton Fair, pre-Christmas), how deposit timing starts the clock
- **Incoterms** — FOB / CIF / DDP and what each shifts onto the buyer; why FOB
  is the office-furniture default
- **Container loading** — 20GP vs 40GP vs 40HQ capacity, why knock-down (KD)
  packing multiplies units per container, CBM per unit
- **Certification by market** — BIFMA X5.1 (US), EN 1335 (EU office chairs),
  ANSI/BIFMA e3, GREENGUARD, FSC for wood, CARB/EPA TSCA Title VI for panels,
  REACH for EU chemicals. **Only name a certification the brand actually holds.**
- **QC and inspection** — in-line vs pre-shipment, AQL sampling, third-party
  (SGS/BV/TUV), what a buyer should specify in the PO
- **Materials** — mesh vs fabric vs PU vs genuine leather; foam density
  (kg/m³) and why it predicts lifespan; nylon vs aluminum base; class 3 vs
  class 4 gas lift; E0/E1 particleboard and MFC vs MDF vs plywood
- **After-sales** — warranty scope, spare-parts supply, replacement part
  shipping with the next order

## 4. Vocabulary

Use: MOQ, OEM/ODM, FOB, CIF, CBM, 40HQ, knock-down (KD), gas lift, syncro-tilt,
lumbar support, BIFMA, EN 1335, foam density, tolerance, batch consistency,
pre-shipment inspection, proforma invoice, T/T deposit.

Avoid: "cutting-edge", "world-class", "one-stop solution", "we are a
professional manufacturer" — buyers read these as noise.

## 5. CTA rules

- **One** soft CTA maximum, and only at the end of the Conclusion.
- Value-first, not sales-first: "Request a spec sheet and current lead times"
  beats "Contact us today!".
- If `cta_style: none` or the brand profile is unfilled, write no CTA.
- Never insert a CTA mid-article and never use exclamation marks.

## 6. Internal linking

Leave 2-4 internal link slots as `[anchor text](/suggested-slug/)` pointing at
the product-category or related-article page the section naturally implies.
Flag them at the end of the output as suggested links so the user can map them
to real URLs. Never invent an external link, statistic source, or citation URL.

## 7. E-E-A-T

The manufacturer's advantage is first-hand production knowledge. Where a
competitor writes "choose a durable chair", write what durability actually is:
the BIFMA cycle test, the foam density, the gas-lift class, the weld points.
Specificity is the ranking edge — but every specific must be true. If a figure
is unknown, give the industry range and say it is a range.
